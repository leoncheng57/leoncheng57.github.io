# Prediction: The Plateau Models

Here's my prediction: **Opus 5.5 and GPT Astra are the plateau models.** Future models will keep getting smarter. But for everyday engineering, I think this is the level where extra intelligence stops paying for itself.

Which makes Fable (Mythos, whatever we're calling it this week) a funny release. It's clearly more powerful, and it's also slow enough that I regret using it about 90% of the time.

## Why the plateau is here

Opus and Astra are smart enough to handle nearly everything I throw at them. They're fast enough that I stay in flow. They also work in chunks small enough that I can follow along.

That last part matters more than it sounds. When a model codes in small steps, I read each diff, push back on the odd choices, and understand the result. At the end, **I can still maintain my own code.**

## The Fable problem

Fable does a lot per turn, and it takes its time doing it. By the time it's finished I've switched to something else, and what comes back is a large, correct-looking diff that I skim and approve. It works, but it isn't really mine. Six months later, debugging it feels like debugging someone else's project.

A smarter model doesn't help if my understanding can't keep up with it.

## Where Fable earns its keep

To be fair, Fable is great at complicated one-shots I'll never need to understand again: a gnarly script, a one-off migration, a proof of concept. For work I'll keep living with, I go back to Opus or Astra.

## Just a prediction

This is a funny prediction of mine and nothing more. I'm not betting anything on it, and I could be completely wrong: maybe next year's Fable is fast and I eat my words. I just wanted to write it down, partly because I might start sharing more funny predictions here. Let's see how this one ages.
